A field course for backend engineers

How data systems actually work, and fail

You already build backends. This course teaches what happens when your data outgrows one machine: the mechanisms underneath replication, transactions and consensus, the failure modes that bite in production, and how to design around them.

How it works

Depth over coverage of buzzwords. Each lesson goes into a mechanism, runs it in an interactive simulator, and ties it to something you can check in your own systems this week. Claims are traced to primary sources (papers, lecture notes, vendor documentation, test reports) and cited in the lesson.

Examples use MySQL, Kafka and AWS, because the best way to learn a mechanism is to see it in the tools you run.

Retrieval over rereading. Every lesson ends with questions that test your ability to apply the idea. Progress is saved in your browser only; nothing leaves your machine.

5 · Retries done right
Timeouts from percentiles, backoff, jitter and retry budgets on AWS. Two labs.

Who it's for

Developers comfortable with SQL, services and production systems who are new to the distributed-systems side. If you've ever been surprised by a duplicate message, a stale read, or a failover that lost writes, this is for you.

Original course material. Concepts come from public sources cited in each lesson. Source code and content are meant to be forked and deployed on GitHub Pages.
