Data Systems Field CourseSyllabus

Syllabus

The full map

Thirteen modules, about 80 lessons, three capstone design reviews. Each lesson is short (20 to 40 minutes), ends with a retrieval check, and names something you can do at work. ✓ built lessons are live; ○ planned ones are scoped here and get built in order.

Order matters: Part I is a single machine, Part II is what breaks when data spans machines, Part III is how to compose systems out of the pieces.

Part 0 · Orientation

Module 0 · The distributed mindset

  1. The timeout problem built — make retries safe with idempotency keys
  2. Partial failure and the "what if this dies right here?" drill — a review technique you can apply to any design doc
  3. System models: crash-stop, crash-recovery, partial synchrony — state your assumptions before claiming correctness
  4. Safety vs liveness — know which property you are actually arguing about
Part I · Foundations on one machine

Module 1 · Measuring systems: reliability, scalability, maintainability

  1. Faults vs failures; designing for fault tolerance
  2. Percentiles, tail latency and why averages lie — build a latency dashboard that tells the truth
  3. Describing load: fan-out on write vs on read (the timeline problem)
  4. Queueing, utilization and why systems fall off a cliff at 80% load
  5. SLIs, SLOs and error budgets — turn "reliable" into a number you can manage
  6. Operability and evolvability: the costs after launch
Part I

Module 2 · Data models and query languages

  1. Relational vs document: locality, joins and the impedance mismatch
  2. Many-to-many and why documents drift toward relations
  3. Schema-on-read vs schema-on-write — choose consciously, not by default
  4. Graph models: property graphs and triple stores
  5. Declarative vs imperative queries (and why optimizers can only help one of them)
  6. Lab: model one real workload three ways and compare
Part I

Module 3 · Storage engines and indexes

  1. The world's simplest database: an append-only log and a hash index
  2. Memtables, SSTables and the LSM-tree
  3. Compaction strategies; read, write and space amplification — predict where an LSM store will hurt
  4. B-trees, pages and the write-ahead log
  5. B-tree vs LSM: a decision framework, not a religion
  6. Secondary, covering and composite indexes; reading an EXPLAIN plan
  7. In-memory databases and what durability then means
  8. OLTP vs OLAP; warehouses and why they're separate
  9. Column storage, compression, vectorized execution
  10. Materialized views and data cubes
Part I

Module 4 · Encoding and evolution

  1. Why JSON, XML and CSV are harder than they look (numbers, binary, schemas)
  2. Schema-based binary formats: field tags vs writer/reader schema resolution
  3. Forward and backward compatibility and rolling upgrades — deploy without a flag day
  4. Dataflow through databases, services (REST/RPC) and message brokers
  5. Lab: evolve a schema through three versions with old and new readers
Part II · Distributed data

Module 5 · Replication

  1. Single-leader replication; synchronous, asynchronous, semi-synchronous
  2. Failover and its hazards: split brain, lost writes, bad timeouts
  3. What actually travels in the replication log
  4. Replication lag anomalies: read-your-writes, monotonic reads, consistent prefix — find them in your product
  5. Multi-leader: when it earns its complexity; conflict handling
  6. Leaderless replication: quorums, read repair, hinted handoff, sloppy quorums
  7. Concurrency without a clock: version vectors and siblings
  8. CRDTs and the idea of merge-by-construction
  9. Lab: break a quorum on purpose
Part II

Module 6 · Partitioning

  1. Key-range vs hash partitioning
  2. Skew and hot keys; mitigation patterns
  3. Secondary indexes: local vs global (document- vs term-partitioned)
  4. Rebalancing strategies and consistent hashing
  5. Request routing and service discovery
Part II

Module 7 · Transactions

  1. ACID without the marketing
  2. Dirty reads, dirty writes and read committed
  3. Snapshot isolation and MVCC internals
  4. Lost updates and the five ways to prevent them
  5. Write skew and phantoms — the bug class that survives code review
  6. Two-phase locking and its costs
  7. Serializable snapshot isolation; actual serial execution
  8. Lab: run isolation-level tests against a real database (Hermitage-style)
  9. Choosing an isolation level for a real feature
Part II

Module 8 · The trouble with distributed systems

  1. Unreliable networks and network partitions in practice
  2. Time-of-day vs monotonic clocks; why timestamps lie
  3. Process pauses (GC, VM stalls) and how they break leases
  4. Leases, fencing tokens and distributed locks done right
  5. Truth by majority; Byzantine faults in one lesson
Part II

Module 9 · Consistency and consensus

  1. Linearizability: what it is and what it costs
  2. The CAP theorem, stated honestly — why "CP or AP" is the wrong question
  3. Ordering, causality and Lamport timestamps
  4. Total order broadcast and why it is consensus in disguise
  5. Distributed transactions: two-phase commit and its blocking problem
  6. Raft, step by step (election, replication, safety)
  7. Coordination services in practice (etcd, ZooKeeper-style)
  8. Reading the Jepsen consistency map — evaluate a database's claims
Part III · Derived data

Module 10 · Batch processing

  1. Unix pipelines as the design ancestor
  2. MapReduce and distributed filesystems
  3. Joins in batch: sort-merge, broadcast, partitioned hash
  4. Dataflow engines and fault tolerance by recomputation
Part III

Module 11 · Stream processing

  1. Logs as the unifying abstraction; brokers and consumer offsets
  2. Change data capture and the outbox pattern — keep two stores in sync without dual writes
  3. Event sourcing and CQRS: benefits and sharp edges
  4. Windows, event time vs processing time, late data
  5. Stream joins and state
  6. Fault tolerance and exactly-once effect in streams
Part III

Module 12 · Correctness and where this is going

  1. The end-to-end argument for data systems
  2. Unbundling the database; derived state as a dataflow
  3. Auditing, integrity checks and trust but verify
  4. Data, privacy and responsibility
Capstones

Design reviews with real systems

  1. Review a payments ledger design (idempotency, transactions, reconciliation)
  2. Review a social timeline (fan-out, caching, partitioning, lag anomalies)
  3. Review your own system at work, with the course's checklists
