Module 5 · Lesson 34

Failover: choose a history, fence a writer

A failover chooses which history becomes authoritative. A timeout does not tell you which history is complete or whether the old writer has stopped.

~35 min1 interactive4-question check
What you can do after this lessonSeparate candidate selection, received-history recovery, fencing and client routing; distinguish temporarily missing acknowledged data from destruction of every copy.

1. Two independent questions

After losing contact with A, a controller must decide which history to serve and who may still write. Promoting a fresh follower does not stop A. Stopping A does not make a stale follower fresh. A timeout only creates suspicion, as Lesson 3 demonstrated.

A: acknowledged [1,2,3]
B: received     [1,2,3]     applied [1,2]
C: received     [1,2]       applied [1,2]

A controller that compares visible rows alone can mistake B and C for equivalent candidates. B has a recoverable tail that C never received. MySQL’s failover documentation treats replication coordinates and source switching as explicit work, not a consequence of changing a hostname.[1]

Try: promote C first. Then select B and apply its received tail before serving. Finally fence A. Observe that recovering transaction 3 and eliminating dual writers require different actions.

2. Acknowledged does not mean present on every candidate

In our real MySQL experiment, we stopped C’s replication before inserting row 4 on A. A acknowledged it and B applied it. We then made stale C writable. Row 4 was absent on C but present on B. Data missing from the newly chosen authority was therefore still recoverable elsewhere; it had not been destroyed on every disk.

If clients observe absence and issue replacements, recovery becomes more than copying a missing row: later writes may now conflict. Preserve evidence before reconnecting divergent branches. Do not assume an automatic catch-up operation will choose the application’s intended history.

3. Split brain is an authority failure

We deliberately left A writable after promoting C. A accepted row 5; C accepted row 6. The resulting ID sets were A: {1,2,3,4,5} and C: {1,2,3,6}. These are executed observations from manual source promotion, not an automated failover product test.

Fencing means the old writer can no longer successfully perform the protected writes. A routing update alone is insufficient if cached connections, old workers or another endpoint can still reach it. The mechanism might revoke access, shut the old writer down, or use an authority-enforced epoch. This lab assumes fencing when selected; it does not implement the mechanism. Leases, fencing tokens and election protocols get deeper treatment in Modules 8 and 9.

4. A failover review, in order

  1. State the acknowledged-data loss budget and failure model.
  2. Obtain trustworthy candidate history information; distinguish received and applied state.
  3. Establish exclusive write authority and choose a candidate containing the required history.
  4. Recover/apply that history before serving the corresponding reads.
  5. Move clients, handle uncertain operations and safe retries, then reconcile or rebuild former leaders before reuse.

The exact sequencing belongs to the actual protocol; this list is a review checklist, not a replacement for a vendor procedure. A source that cannot be contacted may still be alive, and a returning source may contain both valuable history and unauthorized later writes.

5. What the model leaves out

Its promotion is instantaneous and injected. It has no voting, leases, leader epochs, simultaneous controllers, network delays or recovery implementation. MySQL coordinates and GTIDs identify history; a GTID by itself is not a consensus decision. The old Kafka lab also exposes unsafe elections, but its simplified ISR rules are not a MySQL failover protocol.

A practical drill should record which writes were acknowledged, what each candidate durably received, what each served after promotion, and whether an old connection can still write. “The endpoint recovered” covers only one of those questions.

Check yourself

Optional: apply the mechanism (20 minutes)

Draw your failover path. Identify the evidence for candidate completeness, the mechanism stopping old writes, the treatment of uncertain client operations, and how a former source is admitted again.

Ask the course agent about any unclear result. Include the topology, acknowledgement contract and exact failure or message sequence.
